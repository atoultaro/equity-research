# PR9F: Initial Structural Stop Inference

Repository: `atoultaro/equity-research`

## 1. Scientific objective

Infer the latent reasoning behind Adam Grimes' **initial published stop prices** using all captured MarketLife setup episodes.

Our working causal model is:

**Setup and price structure → Structural invalidation → Initial stop → Risk assessment → Entry decision**

We hypothesize that Grimes identifies a meaningful structural invalidation level, selects a stop consistent with that structure, and then evaluates whether the resulting risk makes the opportunity attractive.

ATR14 may normalize risk, but it is not assumed to generate the stop price.

This is a scientific inference study, not a stop-price optimization or execution-system project.

Treat these as source-observed classifications, not classifications inferred by our algorithms.

## 5. Distinguish initial stops, entries and current stops

The research must maintain separate concepts:

- Initial published stop.
- Current published stop.
- Source-reported entry reference.
- Entry reference type: AE, AHE or UNKNOWN.
- Source-reported tracked stop-out.
- Actual subscriber execution: UNKNOWN unless independently documented.

For example, the November 2 dashboard showed:

**DUK LONG UNDERWAY**
- Entry reference: 125.12 AHE.
- Current stop: 126.60.
- LAST: 128.38.

**CRWV SHORT UNDERWAY**
- Entry reference: 56.12 AHE.
- Current stop: 55.00.
- LAST: 50.15.

These current stops have moved favorably beyond their reported entry references.

They must not be mistaken for initial stops.

For a known candidate entry, initial risk is:

LONG:

`entry_price - initial_stop`

SHORT:

`initial_stop - entry_price`

Normalize by the decision-time ATR14 when suitable evidence exists.

If the candidate entry is unknown, entry-to-stop risk remains UNKNOWN.

Never substitute LAST or the closing price for an unobserved entry.

The dashboard's TO STOP metric is not the same as original entry risk.

## 6. Competing initial-stop hypotheses

For each initial stop, compare six hypothesis families.

### H1: Confirmed swing structure

The stop protects a meaningful prior swing low for LONG or swing high for SHORT.

Retain PR9E's strict confirmed-pivot detector as a baseline, but recognize that meaningful structure can exist at different scales.

Do not select pivots based on closeness to the published stop.

### H2: Pullback extreme

The stop protects the low of a relevant pullback for LONG or the high for SHORT.

Identify the pullback using market context available before the decision.

### H3: Consolidation boundary

The stop protects the boundary of a meaningful trading range.

A fixed ten-bar high/low is only a descriptive baseline, not proof of consolidation.

### H4: Breakout or breakdown structure

The stop protects a breakout/breakdown signal bar, the preceding base or another identifiable invalidation boundary.

Distinguish a signal candle from the structure that produced it.

### H5: Volatility-compression structure

Investigate whether several days of contracting daily ranges, overlapping candles or declining volatility create a meaningful structural boundary.

For LONG setups, the initial stop may sit below the compression's lowest low.

For SHORT setups, the initial stop may sit above the compression's highest high.

The causal hypothesis is:

**Compression pattern → Structural boundary → Initial stop**

Not:

**ATR multiplier → Initial stop**

Use simple, predeclared compression definitions. Do not optimize window lengths or buffers to reproduce published stops.

### H6: Volatility-derived alternative

Retain ATR-derived or other volatility-based stop construction as a competing explanation.

Do not presume it is Grimes' primary mechanism.

Do not fit arbitrary ATR multipliers to historical stops and call the fit evidence of his policy.

## 7. Reconstruct the pre-decision chart

For every initial stop:

1. Identify its source alert and setup episode.
2. Establish the permissible information cutoff.
3. Reconstruct the available daily OHLCV.
4. Identify candidate setup structures without using the published stop.
5. Record candidate invalidation levels and structural significance.
6. Compare candidates with the published initial stop.
7. Evaluate competing explanations, contradictions and unidentified variables.

Preserve conditional scenarios for uncertain publication timing.

Do not use future bars or pivots that were confirmed only afterward.

Use existing archived market evidence. Missing historical observations should result in explicit UNKNOWN or CONDITIONAL classifications, not a new infrastructure initiative.

## 8. Evaluate explanatory strength

Do not treat a stop lying on the correct side of a candidate boundary as sufficient proof.

Distinguish:

- Geometric compatibility.
- Proximity to the structural boundary.
- Meaningfulness of the identified pattern.
- Contradictory price behavior.
- Multiple plausible interpretations.
- UNKNOWN or insufficient evidence.

Measure stop-to-anchor distance in native price units and ATR14 units.

Use declared descriptive tolerances only if necessary, with sensitivity analysis. Do not optimize thresholds for maximum historical agreement.

For each hypothesis, document:

- Supporting evidence.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Latent assumptions.
- Alternative explanation.
- Discriminating observation.

## 9. Compare setup families

Where source TYPE evidence is available, compare structural-stop explanations for:

- Pullback.
- Tight pullback.
- Nested pullback.
- Breakout.

In particular, investigate whether nested pullbacks use smaller, local invalidation structures within larger trends.

The two new nested SHORT setups, AVGO and GME, offer useful examples.

The fresh SLB LONG setup, following an explicitly reported stop-out of its earlier LONG episode, provides another important case.

However, do not infer its structural rationale merely from the change between stop prices 52.60 and 51.90.

No universal stop-placement rule should be assumed across setup families.

## 12. Explicit exclusions

Do not:

- Investigate subsequent stop revisions or trailing-stop management.
- Model stop-out mechanics or DROPPED decisions.
- Infer private subscriber executions.
- Replace missing entries with LAST.
- Choose stop levels mechanically from one ATR.
- Optimize a stop-price prediction model.
- Build another acquisition pipeline.
- Require complete external subscriber history.
- Modify operational forecast registration gates.
- Change the execution simulator.
- Rewrite PR9E's historical conclusions.

## 13. Definition of done

PR9F should answer:

1. Which observable price structures plausibly explain the original 34 initial stops and three new November 3 stops?
2. Can multi-day volatility compression explain cases the earlier structural proxies could not?
3. Does Grimes appear to use different invalidation structures for different explicitly identified setup types?
4. What explains the new AVGO, GME and SLB initial stops?
5. Which cases contradict the strongest hypotheses?
6. What information remains latent or unidentified?
7. What observations would distinguish the remaining explanations?

The output is an explanatory scientific study, not a production trading system.
