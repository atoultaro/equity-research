## 2. Scientific objective

Infer the latent reasoning behind Adam Grimes' **initial published stop prices**.

Our working causal model is:

**Trading pattern and market context → Structural invalidation → Protective stop → Risk evaluation → Entry decision**

The protective stop may include room for anticipated volatility expansion.

Crucially, the structural invalidation level and protective stop need not be identical.

ATR14 is used to normalize and evaluate risk. Do not assume that Grimes calculates initial stops from a fixed ATR multiplier.

This is scientific hypothesis investigation, not a production trading system.

## 3. Grimes' own trading patterns are the theoretical foundation

Use Grimes' published article:

https://www.adamhgrimes.com/fundamental-trading-patterns/

And the user-provided excerpts from *The Art and Science of Technical Analysis*, particularly:

- Chapter 3: Simple and Complex Pullbacks.
- Chapter 10: Nested Pullback, including Figure 10.9.

These establish important distinctions.

### Simple pullback

A correction against an established trend.

Candidate invalidation structure: the relevant pullback extreme.

### Complex pullback

A two-legged correction in which an attempted resumption fails and the correction continues.

Candidate invalidation structure may differ from the first pullback extreme.

### Nested pullback

A small consolidation or pullback within a larger driving pattern, often following a strong thrust or breakout.

It is **not synonymous with complex pullback**.

Grimes explicitly discusses using a deeper protective stop because volatility can expand following compression.

Investigate:

- Local consolidation boundary.
- Larger-pattern structural boundary.
- Additional protective room for volatility expansion.

These are competing explanations, not predetermined answers.

### Failure test

Price tests or briefly exceeds a significant level, then reverses.

Investigate whether the failed test's extreme defines invalidation.

### Snap pullback / Anti

A pullback following a potential reversal or sharp directional shift.

Investigate the structure appropriate to that reversal thesis.

### Breakout

A move beyond established support or resistance.

Investigate the breakout signal, preceding base and failure boundary.

**Important:** Grimes' fundamental pattern taxonomy and MarketLife dashboard labels are related, but not necessarily identical.

Do not silently map MarketLife's "Tight pullback" or "Nested pullback" onto another book category.

## 7. Distinguish setup, trigger, entry and stop

Preserve the following separate concepts:

1. NEW SETUP: A published candidate with an initial stop.
2. TRIGGERED: MarketLife reports that an entry condition was met.
3. UNDERWAY: The opportunity has progressed beyond attractive fresh-entry conditions.
4. ENTRY REFERENCE: A reported approximate or actual reference price.
5. STOPPED OUT: MarketLife reports a tracked stop-triggered outcome.
6. DROPPED: A setup or tracked idea is removed, not necessarily because its stop was hit.

Do not infer private subscriber transactions.

For the initial-stop study, prioritize the stop attached to NEW SETUP.

Do not substitute a later triggered or underway stop.

For any known entry reference:

LONG initial risk = entry reference − initial stop.

SHORT initial risk = initial stop − entry reference.

If the entry reference was not available at the initial decision, distinguish retrospective realized/reference risk from information available when the setup was first published.

Never use LAST as a fabricated entry price.

## 8. Initial-stop hypotheses

Compare pattern-conditioned structural explanations.

### H1: Swing invalidation

The stop protects a meaningful prior swing high or low.

The PR9E strict confirmed-pivot detector remains one baseline, not the definition of all significant swings.

### H2: Pullback invalidation

The stop protects the extreme of the relevant simple pullback.

### H3: Complex pullback invalidation

The stop reflects the possibility of a second countertrend leg or protects a completed multi-leg correction.

Do not confuse a complex pullback with a nested pullback.

### H4: Breakout/failure structure

The stop protects the breakout base, signal region or failed-test extreme.

### H5: Nested pullback and volatility compression

A small consolidation occurs within a larger driving pattern.

Compare:

- Local consolidation extreme.
- Larger pattern's structural invalidation level.
- A deeper protective stop accommodating anticipated volatility expansion.

Do not equate volatility compression with an ATR-based stop formula.

### H6: Volatility-derived alternative

Consider a volatility-based stop independent of a recognizable structural boundary.

Keep it as an alternative hypothesis, without fitting an arbitrary ATR multiplier.

These hypotheses can overlap. An observed stop may remain compatible with multiple mechanisms.
