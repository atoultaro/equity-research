# Adam Grimes' blog teachings: setups, entries, initial stops, and trade management

Primary-source reading notes, checked November 5, 2026. This document summarizes Grimes' published blog and podcast *show notes*. It is not a mechanical specification of MarketLife's private decision process, a complete transcription of the textbook, or an evaluation of trading profitability. Each source is linked in the register below. The original articles can describe different trading styles or different periods of Grimes' practice.

## Read this first: SETUP is not an executed trade

**Grimes' direct statements.** In his 2024 MarketLife article, Grimes says the service publishes long/short candidate **setups with initial stops**, discusses *entry points and techniques* in daily videos, and marks candidates **UNDERWAY** when they have moved beyond their entry regions [S01]. His 2018 CHFJPY account describes putting a bull flag on a watchlist with an entry trigger, *waiting*, and reviewing at the end of each daily session whether the setup remains valid, whether the trigger should change (usually no), and **where the initial stop should be** [S02].

**Interpretation for this repository.** A published stop for an ENTERABLE **SETUP** is a proposed protective reference for a prospective *new entry*, evaluated against the current pattern. As new daily bars arrive, that reference can change because the contemplated opportunity has changed. It is **not** evidence that Grimes widened/tightened the resting stop of a single executed personal position. A published stop on a still-monitored **UNDERWAY** item is not by itself a renewed entry invitation; source eligibility governs. Source STOPPED OUT, TRIGGERED, DROPPED and ENTERABLE events must retain their distinct meanings as defined in [the setup/opportunity model](../MARKETLIFE_SETUP_AND_OPPORTUNITY_MODEL.md).

**Separate process.** Post-entry trade management (moving protection on a position, partial profits, targets, time stops) begins only **after** an assumed or observed execution. Grimes' 2012 USDJPY example concerns an *entered position* [S11]; his 2015 never-increase-initial-risk rule concerns an *entered position* [S12]. Do **not** use either to interpret every change in a SETUP's prospective initial stop.

## 1. Recognize the applicable trade family before analyzing prices

Grimes presents a **repertoire of strategies**, not a single universal stop or entry rule [S03, S14].

| Pattern or family | What distinguishes it | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple pullback | A countertrend correction within a potentially continuing trend | Look for the active trend, quality of correction, and return of directional pressure [S03, S04]. |
| Complex pullback | Trend-resumption attempt fails; a second or later countertrend leg develops | The first correction's extreme is not necessarily the final one [S03]. |
| Nested pullback | Small pause *within an active larger driving pattern* | Larger pattern gives the pause its motivation; not synonymous with complex pullback or arbitrary range contraction [S05, S06]. |
| Failure test | Price probes a known level, fails, and reverses | Rejection/test structure rather than generic consolidation is central [S03]. |
| Anti / snap pullback | First pullback after a potentially significant trend change/shock | Different from an ordinary mature-trend continuation [S03]. |
| Breakout | Price escapes a meaningful prior support/resistance region | Entering before, at, or after the break has different risks [S03]. |

These distinctions are **source-derived descriptions**; they are not labels inferred for each historical MarketLife alert. Pattern boundaries can overlap. The chapter excerpts separately supplied by the user describe the two-legged complex correction (Chapter 3) and the nested pause after a larger thrust (Chapter 10); those excerpts are preserved in PR9F's evidence and are **not** reproduced or revalidated here.

**Practical reading:** begin with the *actual labeled setup* when MarketLife supplies a TYPE. If the label is unknown, retain a small set of contextually plausible interpretations or UNKNOWN. Do not run every unrelated pattern detector on every trade and interpret the resulting aggregate match rates as estimates of Grimes' strategy use.

## 2. Entry methods are flexible; trade context is not optional

The 2015 *Pulling the Trigger* show notes emphasize identifying context before choosing an entry. They give examples of a **previous-bar breakout, lower-timeframe entry, and previous close**, and place greater importance on risk, exits and subsequent management [S07]. These are illustrative **alternatives**, not a universal three-step trigger.

The 2021 AUDUSD article analyzes the tradeoff among entering near a flag's bottom, on its breakout, above a larger prior pivot, or on a smaller reversal/pivot break [S08]. Better location can expose a trader to more failed entries; waiting for confirmation can produce a less favorable price. Grimes particularly cautions against indiscriminately buying a new high above the pivot that initiated the flag in stock/index markets. He does **not** advocate buying every previous-bar high break. He also describes a **nested pullback after a larger pattern resolves** as an opportunity to combine confirmation with a better entry location [S08].

For the 2018 CHFJPY bull-flag setup, a trigger above consolidation stayed under observation from September 5 until it occurred; Grimes emphasizes patient reassessment rather than repeatedly chasing the trigger or forcing early participation [S02]. In a separate 2018 GBPCHF short, a break of the previous day's low or a downward trendline supplied workable entry alternatives [S15]. In the 2024 Alcoa example, a green-bar close put a bull flag on the candidate list, and the following day's break above that bar's high triggered entry [S01]. **These are examples, not one invariant MarketLife entry formula.**

**Inference boundary:** plausible structural entries may be examined conditionally alongside the published stop. Exact private brokerage fills and verified entry timestamps are **not prerequisites** for understanding candidate trade geometry. Preserve ordinary no-lookahead discipline and label assumptions; do not backsolve an entry from a desired ATR risk multiple.

## 3. A SETUP's initial stop is assessed against its pattern

In his 2014 pullback article, Grimes teaches identifying a trend likely to continue, entering on its correction, and allowing some adverse motion because the correction may continue. For **pullback trades** he discusses placing a stop beyond a relevant preceding corrective extreme [S04]. This teaching **does not imply** that failure tests, Antis, breakouts and nested setups share one pivot-stop formula.

For **nested pullbacks**, Grimes says the small pause occurs during a larger pattern already in force, and warns that apparently appealing tight stops can fail because of market noise [S05]. The user-supplied textbook Chapter 10 example adds a distinct teaching: after contraction, potential volatility expansion can justify a **deeper protective stop** than the small pause alone suggests, and one may exit upon decisive pattern violation without waiting for that stop. The book passage is *user-supplied textbook evidence*, not a claim made by [S05] verbatim.

The 2011 article *Small Stops, Big Risks?* makes a probability argument: a smaller nominal stop loss can be hit more frequently; the relevant risk calculus includes both loss size and likelihood [S09]. In the 2016 exits article, Grimes also emphasizes controlling total account risk with **position size** instead of assuming very tight stops are inherently safer [S10].

**Important historical nuance:** the 2014 pullback article offers roughly **2–4 ATR from entry** as a *rough starting point for that style* [S04]; the 2016 exits article says his personal initial stops *often* ended up **3–4 ATR from entry** [S10]. These dated statements should not be silently replaced by the user's separate report that contemporary Grimes guidance favors roughly **0.8–1.4 ATR**, with concern above about **2 ATR**. The latter is user-supplied interpretation, not a verified quotation from these linked posts. Do **not** derive any entry or stop from any of these bands. The books/posts and MarketLife observations may concern different instruments, timeframes, styles or periods; why the reported ranges differ is not established.

At the SETUP assessment date, the right inquiry is: *Which price structure matters for this specific opportunity; what would damage the thesis; how much price fluctuation might the prospective protection need to tolerate; and what risk would plausible entries create?* This paragraph is **repository synthesis**, not a deterministic recipe claimed by Grimes.

## 4. Every new daily bar can change the current SETUP assessment

[S02] directly supports scheduled reassessment of **untriggered** setups. A stable candidate can remain on a watchlist until the entry condition occurs; its entry trigger usually should not be chased. At each review, Grimes explicitly reconsiders the initial stop [S02].

This is the central distinction for the source ledger:

| Published event | What we can say | What we must not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NEW SETUP | A proposed opportunity with a published initial stop | That anybody executed it |
| STOP MOVED while ENTERABLE | The proposed protective reference for the current entry opportunity changed | That an actual held position's stop was widened or trailed |
| TRIGGERED | MarketLife reports its tracked entry condition occurred | One exact execution or universal trigger technique |
| UNDERWAY | Candidate progressed too far for a fresh attractive entry under current source state | That all traders entered, profited, or should enter at current stop |
| STOP MOVED while UNDERWAY | The monitored published reference changed | That the SETUP automatically became newly enterable, or that it records a personal broker order |
| DROPPED | The setup/opportunity was removed | An actual liquidation or stop-loss exit |
| STOPPED OUT | Source explicitly reports a stopped-out outcome | A complete personal execution history |

The distinctions of source state and subscriber execution are established in [the opportunity model](../MARKETLIFE_SETUP_AND_OPPORTUNITY_MODEL.md). Specific mechanism claims, such as “this revised stop tracks a newly confirmed swing,” require price-structure evidence *for that setup family*. Directional counts of published stop moves do **not** establish Grimes' executed trade management.

## 5. Trade management **after entry** is a separate subject

These articles describe what Grimes teaches about **held trades**, not what a MarketLife SETUP update necessarily means.

- **Know the loss and risk before entering.** The 2016 article distinguishes initial-loss exits, reduced-loss exits, profit targets and trailing stops; it treats position sizing, entries and exits as one coherent plan [S10].
- **Do not increase accepted risk on an entered trade.** His 2015 order-types article states his practice of entering and placing a protective stop defining 1R, which he does not move farther into risk or cancel [S12]. This is **not** a prohibition on revising the protective level for a fresh *unentered* setup.
- **Respond to expectation changes.** In an actual USDJPY long, he held a relatively stable stop while the anticipated upside breakout had not developed and a complex consolidation was possible; once a sharp breakout occurred, he tightened protection because minimal backtracking was now expected [S11].
- **Consider time and failure to follow through.** The exits article discusses reduced-loss or time-based exits when a position fails to move sufficiently in its expected direction [S10].
- **Trailing techniques are choices, not universal laws.** His 2018 article surveys pivots, very recent highs/lows, wide-range bars, Donchian-type levels, volatility tools and position/holding-period tradeoffs [S13]. It explicitly focuses on *trailing stops after a trade is developing*.
- **Targets and partial exits vary by style and time.** A 2018 GBPCHF SHORT example used an initial 1R target, tightened protection to entry at about +0.8R, took profit at the target and was then stopped at entry on the remainder [S15]. The 2016 article favored target-plus-trail combinations in many swing situations [S10]. In 2022 Grimes reported moving more toward **full 1R wins** after research with Tom, saying this improved results and smoothed the equity curve *for their tested strategies* [S16]. These are dated, style-specific teachings, not proof of a single permanent partial-exit percentage.
- **Failed additions call for risk reduction.** His 2015 article advises reducing an existing directional position by more than the newly added amount when the addition fails, while explicitly noting exceptions for differently planned methods [S17].
- **Different trade families can have different management.** His 2023 *How I Trade* article distinguishes with-trend continuation from countertrend/mean-reversion opportunities and stresses the importance of the initial final stop even in strategies that may add at a worse price [S14].

This is a **different evidence layer** from the MarketLife SETUP ledger. Do not assert that 2026 proposed-stop changes validate trailing stops, personal profit taking, or Grimes' own brokerage execution.

## 6. Source-backed limits and application

**Directly supported by the articles:** multiple pattern families; context-first entry alternatives; daily watchlist reassessment; possibility of updating an *untriggered* setup's initial stop; structural/volatility/noise tradeoffs; separate post-entry loss/target/trailing practices; trading-plan changes across time.

**Not established by the articles:** one algorithm that generates every MarketLife stop, one required entry trigger, a universal ATR multiplier, a mapping from every 2026 alert to an executed position, or a universal stop-revision rule.

**Recommended use when reading one alert:** (1) source setup family if given; (2) contemporaneous context and thesis; (3) plausible entry methods and whether source still deems the opportunity attractive; (4) pattern-relevant initial stop rationale; (5) entry-to-stop risk across plausible entries; (6) only if studying a *hypothetically entered trade*, separately ask about exits and management. Do not infer a setup's pattern from whichever unrelated detector most closely fits its published stop. This is a **research reading method**, not a claim of Grimes' exact hidden policy.
